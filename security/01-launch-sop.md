# SOP 01: Gaming Membership Launch Procedure

**Owner:** Launch Lead  
**Author:** Manus AI  
**Status:** Ready for adaptation  
**Objective:** Prepare a premium gaming membership or mod-access service for launch with a clear offer, reliable onboarding, and a conversion-ready customer journey.

## Purpose

This SOP defines the launch process for a gaming membership offer built around fast setup, a 21-day free trial, and a $200 annual membership. The procedure is designed to make the offer feel polished, trustworthy, and easy to understand before any public promotion begins.

The launch should not depend on hype alone. It should prove value quickly, explain the membership clearly, and remove uncertainty from the user's first decision.

## Launch Standard

> **Launch standard:** Every visitor should understand what the service does, how quickly they can start, what the free trial includes, when the paid membership begins, and why the annual plan is worth keeping.

| Requirement | Standard | Pass Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Offer clarity | The main offer is visible in one sentence | A new user can explain it after one read |
| Setup promise | Setup time is realistic and specific | The onboarding flow supports setup in under one hour |
| Trial terms | The 21-day free period is transparent | Billing and cancellation language is easy to find |
| Annual value | The $200/year offer feels concrete | Benefits are listed in customer-centered language |
| Support readiness | Users know where to get help | Support channel and expected response process are defined |

## Procedure

### 1. Define the Launch Offer

The launch team should use one primary offer statement across all public pages, social posts, and onboarding screens. Consistency matters because customers are more likely to trust an offer when every touchpoint says the same thing.

**Approved primary offer:**

> **"Get set up in under an hour, enjoy 21 days of free access, then keep your membership active year-round for just $200."**

A shorter version can be used in banners, buttons, or social previews:

> **"Start free for 21 days. Keep full-year access for $200."**

### 2. Prepare the Launch Page

The launch page should answer five questions in order: what it is, who it is for, how fast it starts, what the trial includes, and why the annual plan is valuable. The page should avoid vague words such as "best," "ultimate," or "revolutionary" unless they are supported by specific benefits.

| Page Section | Required Message | Performance Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | Fast setup, 21-day free access, $200 annual plan | Immediate comprehension |
| Benefits | Access, guidance, updates, convenience, community or support | Value recognition |
| How it works | Sign up, set up, explore, decide | Reduced friction |
| Trust | Transparent terms, cancellation, support | Lower perceived risk |
| Call to action | Start free membership | Clear next step |

### 3. Verify Operational Readiness

Before launch, the team should simulate a complete user journey from sign-up to first value moment. If the service cannot reliably create value in the first hour, the public promise should be adjusted or the onboarding process should be improved before promotion.

| Test | Required Result |
|---|---|
| Sign-up test | Account or membership access can be created without confusion |
| Setup test | A new user can complete required setup steps in under one hour |
| Content test | The user can identify what to do first |
| Support test | The user can find help without searching extensively |
| Conversion test | The annual offer appears before the trial ends without feeling hidden |

### 4. Launch With Controlled Confidence

The first launch should be controlled rather than chaotic. A small, high-intent audience is more useful than a large unqualified audience because feedback will be clearer and easier to act on.

The launch lead should track the following qualitative signals: confusion, repeated support questions, drop-off during setup, enthusiasm after first use, and objections to the $200/year price. These signals should guide the next revision of the service and content.

## Quality Control

A launch is considered ready when the customer journey is clear, the first-hour experience is dependable, and the annual offer is presented with confidence and transparency.

| Decision | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Go | Setup flow works, content is clear, support is prepared |
| Hold | Trial terms are unclear, setup is inconsistent, or support is missing |
| Revise | Users understand the offer but do not see enough value to continue |
